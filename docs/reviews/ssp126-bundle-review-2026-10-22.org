** ssp126 against ssp245 (26 shared models)

Heat ratio = ssp126 heat deaths / ssp245 heat deaths, global.

- Decade means of the yearly global heat ratio: 1.018 (2022-2030), 1.015
  (2031-2040), 0.965 (2041-2050). Cold: 0.995, 0.995, 1.004.
- 2041-2050 total, per model: heat ratio 0.90 to 1.05, below 1 in 19 of
  26 models. Cold ratio 0.97 to 1.03, above 1 in 15 of 26.
- Per location, median over models of the 2041-2050 heat ratio: median
  0.91, interquartile range 0.88 to 0.95. The lowest ratios (Denmark 78
  0.68, Iceland 83 0.70, Rwanda 185 0.74, Sweden 93 0.75, Chile 98 0.77)
  are on small heat burdens (under 10 heat deaths per year in ssp245).
- Ordering, 2041-2050 heat, 24 models common to all four: ssp126 is below
  ssp245 in 75 % of (location, model) pairs and below ssp585 in 92 %;
  ssp126 is the lowest of the four scenarios globally in 11 of 24 models;
  ssp126 < ssp245 < ssp585 holds in 17 of 24.
- 2022 heat ratio 0.977, cold 1.001; near 1 as expected.
- By cause, 2041-2050, heat ratios lie between 0.95 and 1.03 for every
  cause except =cvd_cmp= (0.67), which, as in the other scenarios, is a
  ratio of near-zero totals and carries no information.

** Early years: ssp126 heat above ssp245 until about 2036

From 2024 to 2036 the global heat ratio is mostly above 1 (even-year values
0.99 to 1.07; for example 1.038 in 2024, 1.067 in 2028, 1.044 in 2036), and
it falls below 1 from about 2038. In 2026-2035 the per-location median ratio
is 0.99, but several large heat-burden locations sit above 1: Bangladesh
(161) 1.10, Nigeria (214) 1.05, China (6) 1.02; India (163) is 1.00.

This is consistent with what I recall (not re-checked) from IPCC AR6: the
SSPs barely differ in global warming before about 2040, and SSP1-2.6's
fast cut in aerosol emissions removes some cooling early on, which could
explain slightly more early warming over polluted regions in South and East
Asia. It is also partly noise: in any single year only 46 % to 69 % of
models have ssp126 above ssp245, so the year-to-year values swing (2028:
1.067, 2032: 0.994). I have not checked the temperatures themselves.

** Size of the mid-century gap

By 2041-2050, ssp126 heat is 3.5 % below ssp245, while ssp585 was 12.8 %
above (2026-10-20 review). The asymmetry may be fine: my recollection of
AR6 (unverified) is that by 2041-2060 SSP1-2.6 is about 0.3 C below
SSP2-4.5 while SSP5-8.5 is about 0.4 C above, 2041-2050 sits early in that
window when the gap is still smaller, and heat risk curves are convex, so
an equal temperature change moves heat deaths more on the upside. I have
not quantified any of these. The population-weighted temperature
difference per location, which the Workflow B summarizer prints, would show
directly whether the small gap comes from the temperatures.
