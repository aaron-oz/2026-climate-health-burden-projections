* Summary

Caspar re-ran the Workflow B summarizer on 2026-10-29 with per-draw exports
(=docs/ops/caspar-workflow.md= section 10b). Workflow B rescales each target
scenario's (ssp126, ssp370, ssp585) burden to IHME's mortality forecasts, with

(the folder name is misleading: it holds the whole delivery; the exports are
in its =summary_workflow_b_v2/=).

Result: every mechanical check on the export passed, and the pooled numbers
match what we had written down in advance, with one wrong expectation of mine
(cold deaths rise; I expected roughly flat) and one wrong guess about which
cause ranks second for cold. No re-run is needed from Caspar. The review did
surface things that affect how results are presented; none looks like a bug:

1. The disaster-forecast tail reaches beyond Haiti (Honduras, and single draws
   in Puerto Rico and Myanmar). At the decade level, among countries with more
   than 10 non-optimal deaths per year, only Haiti and Honduras move by more
   than about 5%.
2. Many single-cause, single-country intervals include zero (60% for heat, 31%
   for cold, ssp585, 2041-2050). The team's priority breakdown is by cause.
3. Cold deaths and cold YLLs (years of life lost) can have opposite signs: 8 to
   22 countries have negative pooled cold YLLs with positive cold deaths under
   ssp585, depending on decade.
4. IHME's forecast multiplies 17-cause deaths by 10.6 in the United Arab
   Emirates between 2022 and 2050, and by 3 to 8 in other Gulf states, which
   puts Gulf states at the top of the heat rate table for 2041-2050.
5. =util_pool_workflow_b_draws.R= cannot yet form decades from the cause and
   age blocks.

All of these are from this delivery only. I did not read Caspar's source files
(=wb_*.rds=); agreement with them rests on his summarizer's own hard checks.

Rounding note. Rounding moves each value by at most 5e-6 of itself. Measured
against max(|value|, 1), the largest disagreements with the CSVs were up to
1.1e-3, larger than that. They sit where draws of both signs nearly cancel. I
tested the worst locations against the correct bound: for cause means (Burkina
Faso, Thailand, Bangladesh), worst deviation / mean(|draw|) is 8.1e-7 against
the bound of 5e-6; for lower quantiles (Bangladesh, Indonesia, Thailand, Burkina
Faso), worst deviation is 0.92 of the bound computed from the two order
statistics. I tested those locations only, not all 204.

to deaths of mixed sign across draws or years; I did not verify that row by
row. For 15-49 the cold ratio is out of range in 37,618 of 82,273 rows, which
is the sign problem discussed below, not a useful check.

* Points that affect how results are presented

** The disaster-forecast tail

Mechanism (documented for Haiti, =docs/reviews/ssp245-pilot-review-2026-09-14.org=):
some IHME =inj_disaster= forecast draws carry event-scale deaths. A PAF
(population attributable fraction) of a percent or two on those deaths swings
the total; in Haiti the event also drags the derived TMREL (theoretical minimum
risk exposure level) to the floor in those draws. All 17 causes stay in, as
the team decided on 2026-09-14.

Non-optimal deaths per year, ssp245, 2041-2050, pooled, with and without the
=inj_disaster= cause itself, side by side (this removes that cause's own deaths,
not its effect on TMRELs, so it does not undo the Haiti floor). Eight countries
have an upper bound moved by more than 2%, two by more than 10%.

Single-year and single-draw effects are larger than these decade figures. In
the yearly totals, the highest value reaches 19,041 in Puerto Rico (draw 209 in
every model: 2042 and 2048 are its only years with mean heat above 200; heat 2042
averages 3,491 against 124 in other draws, mostly =inj_disaster=), 81,968 in
Myanmar (median 11,516) and 115,656 in Haiti (median 539). This is another reason
to avoid single-year country values. Effect on World: Haiti's mean is about 0.07%
of the World net total for ssp245, 2041-2050. I did not check TMRELs for Puerto
Rico or Myanmar; the tail could be the cause's own deaths only.

Honduras's ranking among wide intervals is only moderate (relative width
(upper - lower) / mean = 0.85, ssp585, 2041-2050; median over countries 0.78).
Haiti is first (3.92). Second is Singapore (3.29), which has a different cause:
lower respiratory infection is 86% of its mean (260.6 of 301.7, ssp585) with a
cause interval of -125.6 to 1,415.4, and the same draws lead in every model, so
this appears to come from the exposure-response draws for that cause in a hot
climate. I have not looked further. Malaysia (1.88) has a wide interval spread
over several causes.

** Intervals by cause, country level

Share of (cause, country) cells whose 95% interval includes zero, ssp585,
2041-2050: heat 60.2% of 3,452 non-zero cells, cold 30.7%. By cause it runs from
95% (hypertensive heart disease, heat) to 0% (homicide and suicide, cold). At
World level under ssp585, 9 of 17 causes for heat and 1 of 17 for cold include
zero. So
country-by-cause tables will mostly show "cannot distinguish from zero" for
heat. This is the exposure-response uncertainty, not something the export
introduced. Plain country totals rarely have this problem: for deaths, at most
2 of 204 countries include zero.

** Negative cold YLLs alongside positive cold deaths

Pooled over ssp585: negative mean cold YLLs in 22 countries (2022-2030), 12
(2031-2040) and 8 (2041-2050); 95% interval includes zero in 58 to 68. Cold
deaths have no negative means and include zero in only 1 or 2 countries.
Example: Burkina Faso, ssp126, 2041-2050: cold deaths +114.6, cold YLLs -294.9.
Reason, as far as I can tell: injury causes at young ages (high remaining life
expectancy) have negative cold values and outweigh small positive cold deaths
at old ages. It follows that YLLs per death is not a usable statistic wherever
signs mix (it shows 110.7 for men 15-49 at World level in 2022-2030).

** IHME's forecast in the Gulf states

17-cause deaths, 2022 to 2050 (same across models and scenarios): United Arab
Emirates 10,961 to 116,058 (x10.6), Qatar x8.2, Oman x4.3, Bahrain x4.1, Kuwait
x4.1, Saudi Arabia x3.2; World x1.52, median country x1.60. IHME's population
65+ in the United Arab Emirates goes from 198,137 to 4,717,387. This is consistent
with migrant workers being forecast to age in place, which is my guess and not
something the files state. These are IHME's inputs and the pipeline follows them;
it does mean Gulf heat rates in 2041-2050 rest on that forecast. Earlier I saw a
"cold spike" in the United Arab Emirates in 2050; it is this trend, not a spike.
