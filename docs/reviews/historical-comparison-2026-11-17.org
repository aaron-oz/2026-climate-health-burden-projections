  Years 1990, 2010, 2019; deaths and YLLs as Rate (per person; multiplied by

- GBD 2023 round: GBD Results Tool download,
  data/gbd-comparison/gbd_A_anchor_2019_2022.csv (untracked, in the main

- Ours: the 2026-11 pooled results with the WBG groups (24 common climate

* Method
- Measure: crude death rate per 100,000, all ages, both sexes, for heat,
  cold, and heat + cold. Rates sidestep the missing counts in the
  2019-round file and the population growth between 2019 and 2050.
- Ours: world by single year (mean and 95% range of the 12,000 pooled
  values, divided by IHME's mean population that year) and by decade;
  countries and groups by decade (the existing pooled tables).
- GBD groups (world in the 2023 round; income groups and regions in both
  rounds): sum of country counts over sum of population. Means only, no
  ranges, since ranges cannot be added across countries. The 2019-round
  counts are rate x the 2023 round's 2019 population (Number / Rate), so
  2019-round group values exist for 2019 only and carry the two rounds'
  population difference (not measured).
- Deaths only: our YLLs use each country's own life table, so they are not
  comparable with GBD's.
- Code: output/historical-comparison-2026-11/build_historical.R (tables)
  and make_historical_figures.R (figures). Output in
  ~/data/wb-temp-attr-projections/review/historical-comparison-2026-11/:
  world_rates.csv, group_rates.csv, country_rates.csv,
  country_agreement.csv, ours_world_yearly_rates.csv, figures-report/,
  figures-slides/.

Reading, with caveats:
- Our 2022 values sit close to the GBD 2019 round's 2019 values, the round
  whose method we implement, and inside its 95% ranges. Our heat sits below
  the GBD 2023 round, consistent with that round's upward revision of heat
  in hot countries (August review).
- GBD's own heat rate moves from year to year with observed weather (5.27
  in 2010, 4.62 in 2019). Our yearly values come from climate-model
  weather, so a single year of ours is not a forecast of that year's
  weather; decades are the better comparison.
- Crude rates rise in our projection partly because populations age: the
  cold crude rate goes from 17.65 (2022) to 21.63 (2041-2050), while cold's
  share of deaths from the 17 causes stays about flat (4.66% to 4.62%
  between 2022-2030 and 2041-2050, from the 2026-11-09 slides). An
  age-standardized comparison would separate this; the GBD 2019 round has
  age-standardized rates, ours will come from the final summary pass.
