#+TITLE: What drives the rise in heat deaths? A four-part split, 2026-10-27

* Question

Global heat deaths roughly double between 2022 and 2050 in every scenario.
How much of that is population growth, population aging, changing death
rates, and the heat itself?

* Method

For each location, heat deaths H are a sum over age-sex groups c (17 age
groups from 0-4 to 80+, two sexes):

  H = sum_c P * s_c * r_c * f_c

- P: total population of the location.
- s_c: share of the population in group c (age structure).
- r_c: deaths per person in group c from the 17 temperature-related causes
  (IHME's forecast death rates).
- f_c: share of those deaths attributable to heat. This is mostly the
  temperature, but it also moves if the mix of causes within an age group
  changes.

The change in H between two years is split into four additive parts with the
Shapley (Das Gupta) method: each factor's part is its average effect over all
24 orders of switching the factors from the first year to the second, so the
parts sum exactly to the change and no factor is favored by going first.
Done per location, climate model and scenario; world parts are sums of
location parts; reported as the mean over models, as % of the starting
year's world heat deaths.

Inputs: the unscaled scenario summaries' =by_age_sex.csv= (pipeline burden;
the adjustment to IHME's forecasts moves global totals by under 0.15%) and
IHME's population forecast (=~/data/wb-temp-attr-projections/gbd-forecasts/population.csv=,
mean of its draws). Models: the 24 run for all four scenarios. Script:
=output/analysis-heat-decomposition-2026-10-27/decompose_heat.R=; printed
output =2022-2050.txt=, =2022-2040.txt= and CSVs in the folders beside it.

Checks (=check_decomposition.R=, output =check_2022-2050.txt= and
=check_2022-2040.txt=), all passing:
- the 17-cause deaths are identical across climate models (worst relative
  spread 0), as expected, and every location matched a population;
- every location-model heat total in 2022, 2040 and 2050 matches
  =national_by_year.csv= (worst relative deviation 4e-11);
- the four parts sum to the change in every location, model and scenario;
- a separate brute-force average over all 24 orders reproduces the parts
  (8 sampled location-model pairs, including India and China; worst
  difference 1e-14 of the change);
- world population (these 204 locations, IHME forecast) is 7.95 bn in
  2022, 9.03 bn in 2040 and 9.38 bn in 2050; the share aged 65+ rises from
  9.9% to 14.7% and 17.1%.

* Limitation that shapes the comparison

=by_age_sex.csv= holds only 2022, 2030, 2040 and 2050. So this compares
single years (2022 with 2050, and 2022 with 2040), not the decades used
elsewhere (2022-2030 with 2041-2050). A single year carries that year's
weather in each model; averaging 24 models damps it, but the per-model range
of the heat part is wide (below). A decade version needs =by_age_sex.csv=
for every year, which means re-running the unscaled summarizer on Caspar's
machine with =--age_years=2022-2050=.

Parts are percentage points of the 2022 level and sum to the change. 2022
differs a little between scenarios because each scenario's model runs have
their own weather from 2015 on.

Readings (model means; no uncertainty ranges):

- Population aging is the largest single part, about +105 to +117 points
  of a roughly +105 to +140% rise.
- Falling age-specific death rates offset most of it, about -70 to -77.
- Population growth adds about +23 to +25.
- The heat share adds +46 (ssp126) to +78 (ssp585). This is the only part
  that differs much between scenarios; the scenario contrast in heat deaths
  comes almost entirely through it. The other parts differ slightly between
  scenarios because each is evaluated alongside that scenario's heat share.
- Under ssp245, aging contributes about twice as much as the heat share
  (+106 against +47), but the heat share's model range (+14 to +94) is wide.

2022 to 2040 (same table in =2022-2040.txt=): change +57% (ssp245) to +80%
(ssp585); aging +57 to +62, death rates -39 to -42, population +16 to +17,
heat share +23 (ssp245) to +44 (ssp585). ssp126's heat share (+27) is above
ssp245's (+23) in 2040, consistent with its early excess noted on
2026-10-22.

* What this does not show

- No uncertainty ranges; model means only, plus the model range of the heat
  part.
- The heat-share part mixes warming with shifts in cause mix within age
  groups; separating them needs cause-by-age results (Caspar's machine).
- Single years, not decades (see above).
- The split is of the unadjusted pipeline burden, not the Workflow B
  (adjusted) numbers; the two differ by under 0.15% globally.
