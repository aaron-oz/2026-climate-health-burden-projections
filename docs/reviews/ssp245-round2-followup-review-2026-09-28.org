* Finding 2: Honduras's shock tail is the Haiti mechanism, milder

The 2026-09-24 review hypothesized, from summaries only, that Honduras's
heat-side mean-median gap comes from a disaster tail in IHME's
=inj_disaster= forecast pulling the derived TMREL down in a few draws.
The cache allows the direct check.

** The code reproduces the cache

Re-deriving Honduras's 2022 TMRELs locally from the cached =cod= weights
and our 500-draw ERF cache reproduces Caspar's cache exactly (all 11,500
zone-draw values; =r2f_d_rederive.R=). As for Haiti, any surprise is in
the input, not in a code or machine difference.

** A few draws carry an event-scale disaster toll, and those draws lower the TMREL

2022, per draw (=r2f_c_shock_draws.R=). "Share" = =inj_disaster= deaths /
17-cause deaths in that draw; "floored" = TMREL at 6.6 C, the bottom of the
6.6 to 34.6 C search range.

| location  | draws with share > 30 % | their disaster deaths | median disaster deaths (all draws) | their mean TMREL (C) | other draws' mean TMREL (C) | floored zones per shock draw (of 23) |
|-----------+-------------------------+-----------------------+------------------------------------+----------------------+-----------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| Haiti     | 22 (shares 50 % to 87 %) | 179,105 to 304,955   | 35                                 | 10.8                 | 22.4                        | 14.7                                 |
| Honduras  | 17 (30 % to 46 %)        | 14,909 to 26,361     | 10                                 | 15.9                 | 22.7                        | 5.5                                  |
| Nicaragua | 8 (30 % to 38 %), plus 9 at 10 % to 30 % | 4,319 to 7,571 | 12                              | 18.4                 | 22.9                        | 2.4                                  |

(Haiti and Nicaragua from Caspar's pilot cache of 2026-09-11, 2022.)

For Honduras there is no middle ground: 483 draws have share under 2.4 %
and 17 have share 30 % to 46 %; none lie between. Across the 500 draws the
correlation between share and the draw's zone-mean TMREL is -0.97 in 2022,
-0.90 to -0.98 in every year 2022 to 2050 (=r2f_b_honduras_years.R=).

Which zones collapse matters. In Honduras's 17 shock draws the TMREL
falls most in zones 17 to 21 (zone = the pixel's annual mean temperature
in C): mean 8.4 to 13.3 C against 23.0 to 23.9 C over all draws, and 9 to
15 of the 17 draws floored in each of those zones. Zones 22 and 23 also
fall to about 12.4 C but with no draw floored, zones 24 and 25 fall to
20.2 to 21.2 C, and zones 26 to 28 barely move (23.3 to 27.2 C). Haiti's shock draws collapse across most zones. We
have not checked locally which zones Honduras's population lives in (the
local data have no Honduras temperature grid), so the link from "zones 17
to 21 collapse" to "a milder burden effect than Haiti" is plausible but
not verified.

Dropping =inj_disaster= from the weights (the rule rejected globally on
2026-09-14, used here only as a diagnostic) removes all 93 floored
zone-draws and lifts the 17 shock draws to 17.7 to 26.6 C by zone, in
line with the other draws. It also shifts TMRELs in 1,005 zone-draws
outside the shock draws (up to 3.2 C, zone-mean effect over all draws
+0.25 C), so the rule is not free even for normal draws.

** The year-to-year size of the burden gap tracks the number of shock draws

If the shock draws cause the gap, years with fewer of them should show a
smaller gap. The number of Honduras draws with share over 30 % varies by
year (9 in 2034, 10 to 23 elsewhere). Against the 27-model mean of
(heat deaths mean - heat deaths median) from =national_by_year.csv=, per
year 2022 to 2050 (=r2f_e_gap_vs_shocks.R=):

- correlation with the number of shock draws: 0.83 (29 years);
- correlation with the number of floored zone-draws: 0.76;
- 2034, the year with the fewest shock draws (9), has the smallest gap
  (37 deaths, against 57 to 108 in other years);
- the cold side shows no gap (cold mean / median - 1 within +/-0.01 in
  every year).

29 annual points with a trend in both series; the correlations are
suggestive, not a formal test.

** What this settles and what it does not

Settled, from the cache: Honduras's heat-side skew comes from a
2 % to 5 % subset of IHME's forecast draws (9 to 23 of 500 depending on
year) in which =inj_disaster= carries an event-scale toll (2022: 14,909
to 26,361 deaths; the largest draw in any year is 30,804), enough to
drag the death-weighted TMREL toward the floor in the mid-temperature
zones. Same mechanism as Haiti; smaller tail (about a tenth of Haiti's
toll per shock draw) and partial rather than near-total zone collapse.

Also answers the Nicaragua puzzle from 2026-09-24 in part: Nicaragua's
shock draws are smaller in both deaths (4,000 to 7,600) and share (at
most 38 %, 9 of its 17 shock-type draws below 30 %), so fewer zones reach
the floor (2.4 per draw against Honduras's 5.5).

Not settled:
- The exact burden effect of the shock draws. The bundle has no Honduras
  per-draw burden, and we have no Honduras temperature locally, so we
  cannot recompute Honduras without the shock draws as we did for Haiti.
  The summary gap bounds the size: the 2022 27-model means are 525
  (mean) vs 431 (median) non-optimal deaths, heat 241 vs 142.
- Whether the same draw indices are affected across the 27 models
  (true for Haiti). This is expected, since mortality draws are shared
  across models, but it is not visible without per-draw burden. Across
  years the shock draws are mostly different indices (331 distinct
  draws have share > 10 % in some year, most in only 1 or 2 years).
- The historical anchor for IHME's Honduran disaster tail (Hurricane
  Mitch, 1998, is the obvious candidate) remains unchecked.
