* Pre-registered comparison with the 2026-11 pooled results
Script: output/final-summary-pass-2026-11/check_against_2026_11.R
(expectation written before the run: every mean, bound and difference within
a relative 1e-4, relative error = |new - old| / max(|old|, 1)).

Result: 15,624 of 15,624 rows matched. Means, upper bounds and the 80% and
66% bounds all pass (worst mean 5.1e-6). *As written, the check fails on 78
rows*: 1 lower 95% bound and 77 differences from SSP2-4.5 (diff_mean 15,
diff_lower95 34, diff_upper95 28 exceedances; worst 5.4e-3).

What those rows are (output/final-summary-pass-2026-11/review/check_diffs_detail.R):
- Every one is a quantity near zero relative to its own level, so the
  relative error divides by a small number. Example: Middle East, North
  Africa, Afghanistan & Pakistan, 2041-2050, SSP5-8.5, heat YLLs: level
  2.17 million, lower 95% bound of the difference 555.137 (old) vs 555.220
  (new); change 0.08 YLL, which is 4e-8 of the level.
- Measured against the quantity's level, scale = max(|mean|, |lower95|, 1),
  the largest change in any compared column is 1.5e-5.
- The source of the change is consistent with 4-byte float storage in the
  compact copy (relative precision about 6e-8) plus 6-significant-digit
  rounding of the old files, carried into differences of two large
  numbers. I did not trace individual rows to confirm this.
- Judgment: the two routes agree; the pre-registered threshold was
  mis-specified for differences and near-zero bounds, where a relative
  criterion should have been scaled by the level, not the value itself.

* Sanity review (output/final-summary-pass-2026-11/review/sanity_review.R)
All figures are mean annual deaths unless stated.
- Completeness: 30,248,064 rows in the main tables, 139,392 per unit for all
  217 units; no missing means or bounds.
- Additivity of means (max relative difference, rounding level):
  World vs sum of 204 countries 3.7e-6; 7 regions vs World 4.0e-6;
  4 income groups vs World 3.9e-6; low and middle income + high income vs
  World 7.0e-6. Regional populations sum to World's (ratio 0.999999).
- Causes and ages add up: World SSP2-4.5 2022-2050 cold deaths, all causes
  1,749,870 = all but disaster 1,751,690 + disaster -1,821; the 17 age
  groups sum to 1,749,873.
- Decades vs annual table: the average of annual World means equals the
  decade mean (for example 2022-2030 heat 431,244.2 vs 431,244).
- Anchors: World 2022 crude rates under SSP2-4.5 are 4.97 (heat) and 17.65
  (cold) deaths per 100,000, the same values as the historical comparison of
  2026-11-17. The historical table's GBD 2019-round 2019 values are 4.62
  and 17.23 per 100,000 (age-standardized 4.59 and 17.53); our 2022-2030
  age-standardized rates are 4.69 and 15.59.
- Direction by scenario, World 2041-2050: heat 703,149 (SSP1-2.6) < 729,507
  (SSP2-4.5) < 735,317 (SSP3-7.0) < 821,948 (SSP5-8.5); cold lowest under
  SSP5-8.5 (1,950,760; difference from SSP2-4.5 -48,176, 95% interval
  -106,725 to -16,804). In 2022-2030 the scenarios do not order (heat
  439,564, 431,244, 424,074, 438,032), which is expected this early when
  scenario forcing barely differs and model-to-model spread dominates.
- Age standardization goes the expected way: age-standardized rates are
  below crude rates in older populations (high income cold 13.8 vs 33.8)
  and above them in younger ones (low income cold 16.6 vs 5.7). Over time
  World cold crude rates rise (18.1 to 21.6) while age-standardized rates
  fall (15.6 to 11.3), consistent with population aging driving the count.
  The 2022-2050 age-standardized rate is not the average of the three
  decades' rates (cold 12.9 vs about 13.3); this fits rates pooled as
  sum of deaths over sum of population across all 29 years, which weights
  later years more. I did not confirm this in the stage B code.
- Signs: cold deaths and YLLs from injuries are negative in almost every
  unit (World SSP2-4.5 2022-2050 injuries cold -121,822), as the
  injury exposure-response curves imply (injury risk falls with cold).
  All-cause cold YLLs have a negative mean in 213 country rows (hot
  countries where negative injury YLLs at young ages outweigh positive
  disease YLLs); the 2026-11 pooled results had the same pattern.
- Mean outside its own 95% interval in 2,752 of 30.2 million rows, most
  often disaster injuries (1,277). These are skewed draw distributions
  (example: Puerto Rico, SSP1-2.6, 2041-2050, disaster cold YLLs, mean
  45.4, 95% interval -68.1 to 40.2), the same disaster-injury tail seen
  for Haiti in 2026-09.
- Vetting: share of World heat-death variance between climate models 0.42
  to 0.48 by period (cold 0.08 to 0.13); no World heat/cold crossing by
  2050 in any scenario. The temperature table carries all 27 models.
