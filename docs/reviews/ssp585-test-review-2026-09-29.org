2. The burden manifest (=manifests/burden_manifest_ssp585.csv=, one row per

4. Rerunning that combo locally (same code on main, same temperature, the

   The legacy setting reproduces his file exactly. The file also lacks the
   =model= and =scenario= columns that the current ssp245 files carry,
   consistent with an older code version.

Why the check passed: =util_run_provenance.R= ignores "skip" rows when
finding a combo's latest attempt, so a combo that was only ever skipped
looks as if it has no manifest record, and "no record" was reported but
exempt from the =--since= test. Fixed on branch =ssp585-bundle-review=: with
=--since= given, a combo with no "ok" attempt since that time now fails
whether or not it has other rows. Tested on this bundle's manifest: the
check now fails on exactly these 119 combos and the generated
=fix_run.sh= reruns exactly them.

* Plausibility, with the stale combos noted

These readings include the 119 stale combos, so for Haiti, Colombia and
Kuwait they are provisional until the repaired bundle arrives. The other
nine locations have no stale combos.

- P1, temperature (ensemble population-weighted temperature, ssp585 minus
  ssp245). Near 0 in 2022 (-0.33 to +0.05 C), positive by 2040 and 2050 in
  all 12 locations (2050: +0.21 to +0.59 C). Three CHECK flags (Brazil
  135, India 163, Uganda 190) are only that 2040 exceeds 2050 (India +0.44
  vs +0.43 C); with an across-model standard deviation near 0.5 C over 27
  models, single-year ensemble differences of that size are within noise
  (rough reasoning, not a formal test). I read the rule "last year must be
  the largest" as too strict for single years.
- P2, direction (ensemble ssp585 / ssp245 heat and cold deaths, by decade).
  11 of 12 locations as expected: 2041-2050 heat ratios 1.07 to 1.41, cold
  ratios 0.84 to 1.01. Haiti is the CHECK: heat 0.976 in 2022-2040 and cold
  1.027 in 2041-2050. Dropping Haiti's one stale model moves 2022-2040 heat
  to about 1.00 but leaves 2041-2050 cold at 1.029. Splitting by cause
  (26 models, summed over years): without =inj_disaster=, Haiti's 2041-2050
  heat is 1.49 x ssp245 and cold 0.88 x, as expected; =inj_disaster= alone
  goes the other way (heat 0.95 x, cold 1.19 x) and is large (heat
  330,093 vs 109,651 for all other causes combined, sums over models and
  years). This is the known Haiti =inj_disaster= shock tail, the same
  mechanism behind the round-2 asterisk, not a new problem.
- P3, models that warm more add more heat deaths: correlation positive in
  all 12 locations at 2050 (r = +0.27 Haiti to +0.97 Nigeria).
- P4, size of the scaling: ensemble scaled / unscaled within 0.999 to 1.012
  per decade; implied change in IHME mortality -0.06 % to +0.43 %.
- P5, heat share higher under ssp585 in 2041-2050 in all 12 locations.
- P6, uncertainty: median relative 95 % interval widths 0.34 to 1.67,
  except Haiti at 14.6 (shock tail, as in ssp245).

* Decision: combining climate models (Aaron, 2026-09-29)

Draws are already paired correctly within Workflow B: for each climate
model, ssp245 and ssp585 are joined by draw index
(=util_workflow_b_ratio.R=), and draw d uses the same risk-curve draw (the
first 500 of 1,000), the TMREL derived from it, and IHME mortality draw d in
every climate model, so only temperature differs between models at a given
draw. Open question for IHME, not blocking: whether their mortality draw d
was built with risk-curve draw d; if not, the scale factor adds some noise
to intervals rather than cancelling it (size not measured).

For the multi-model summary (not yet built), decided:

- Headline intervals pool all models x draws (27 x 500) and take quantiles
  of that mixture, so disagreement between climate models is part of the
  interval. Alongside, report the range of the 27 per-model means.
- Aggregates across locations (regions, world) keep the climate model fixed
  and sum draw by draw within a model before pooling.
