#+TITLE: Gridded population by scenario: where it comes from and how much it moves the weights
#+DATE: 2026-11-13

Asked by Aaron, 2026-11-13: where the scenario populations come from, how much
the within-country weights differ between scenarios, and what the
inconsistency (national population fixed at IHME's forecast, spatial pattern
following each SSP) does to the results.

* Two populations in the pipeline
- National population by age and sex: IHME's GBD 2021 forecast
  (=~/data/wb-temp-attr-projections/gbd-forecasts/population.csv=). Turns
  forecast death rates into deaths and is the rate denominator. The same in
  all four scenarios.
- Gridded population: CCKP's =gpw-v4-rev11-<ssp>= files, 0.25 degree, 20-year
  means (2020-2039 for 2022-2039, 2040-2059 for 2040-2050), chosen per run by
  =year_to_pop_bucket()= in =global-scripts/util_run_cckp_pipeline.R=. Used
  only as within-country weights: pixel weight pi_p = pixel population /
  location's gridded total (=03_load_temperature.R=). Gridded totals never
  become counts.

* Where the scenario gridded populations come from
- What the files themselves show (read 2026-11-13): the NetCDF metadata names
  only CCKP labels (=wb_model_label= "gpw-v4", =wb_variant_label= "rev11",
  =wb_scenario_label= the SSP) and a bilinear regrid; no source citation.
  CCKP serves seven population sets: historical, ssp119, ssp126, ssp245,
  ssp370, ssp4, ssp585. The ssp119 and ssp126 files for 2040-2059 are
  identical in every pixel (maximum absolute difference 0), so the population
  depends on the socioeconomic pathway (SSP1) and not on the forcing level, as
  SSP population projections do. World totals in the 2040-2059 files: SSP1
  8.43, SSP2 9.13, SSP3 9.95, SSP5 8.53 billion. For comparison, IHME's
  forecast gives 9.24 billion as the 2041-2050 mean (our report).
- What CCKP's documentation says: not read directly. The CCKP metadata page
  (climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/metadata) is behind a browser check
  that blocked our fetch. A web search summary of that page reports that the
  population data are GPWv4 rev 11 (CIESIN/SEDAC) for the baseline, projected
  under the SSPs with age and sex proportions from the IIASA (Wittgenstein)
  human-capital projections by a cohort-component method. This is unverified
  until someone reads the page; how national SSP totals are spread over the
  grid is not stated in what we saw.

* How much the weights differ (all locations)
Script: =output/review-gridded-pop-2026-11-13/weights_by_scenario.R=; output
=~/data/wb-temp-attr-projections/review/gridded-pop-2026-11-13/weights_by_scenario.csv=.
Pixels are assigned to locations by pixel center in polygon, as in the
conversion step. 184 of 204 locations have at least one pixel center; the
other 20 (small islands and microstates) use a single nearest pixel in the
pipeline, so their weights cannot differ between scenarios.

Measures, per location, each scenario s against SSP2-4.5 in the same 20-year
period:
- tv = 0.5 * sum_p |pi_s,p - pi_245,p|, total variation distance: the share
  of people placed in a different pixel (0 identical, 1 disjoint).
- dT = sum_p (pi_s,p - pi_245,p) * T_p, in degrees C, with T_p the annual
  mean daily temperature of one fixed field (access-cm2, SSP5-8.5, 2050): how
  far the reweighting alone moves the population-weighted mean temperature.
- dT_clim = sum_p pi_245,p * (T_ssp585,p - T_ssp245,p), same model and year:
  the climate difference between SSP5-8.5 and SSP2-4.5 with the weights held
  at SSP2-4.5. One model, one year, so a rough yardstick only.

Quantiles over the 184 locations (minimum / median / 90% / 99% / maximum):

| 2020-2039 | SSP3-7.0 | 0.000 / 0.010 / 0.029 / 0.044 / 0.049 | 0.000 / 0.007 / 0.053 / 0.122 / 0.150 |
| 2020-2039 | SSP5-8.5 | 0.000 / 0.009 / 0.025 / 0.035 / 0.037 | 0.000 / 0.006 / 0.041 / 0.123 / 0.195 |

| 2040-2059 | SSP3-7.0 | 0.000 / 0.018 / 0.055 / 0.081 / 0.105 | 0.000 / 0.013 / 0.089 / 0.233 / 0.315 |

SSP3-7.0, 12 under SSP5-8.5.

* What this does and does not show about the burden
- Shown: the weights differ little for most locations, and the reweighting's
  effect on population-weighted mean temperature is small next to the climate
  difference for most of them; a handful of mostly mountainous or
  coastal-to-interior countries are exceptions (Tajikistan, Kyrgyzstan,
  Senegal, Mexico, Zimbabwe).
- Not shown: the effect on attributable deaths. That depends on the daily
  temperature distribution and the risk curves, not on the annual mean, and
  dT can be offset or amplified. Measuring it faithfully means running the
  production burden pipeline with the SSP2-4.5 gridded population swapped in
  for a target scenario and comparing. That needs each location's 17-cause
  mortality for the derived minimum-risk temperature, which is on the
  production machine; this machine has only ischemic heart disease (plus a
  cached minimum-risk temperature for Honduras, where dT is about 0.005 C and
  the test would say little).
- Direction of the inconsistency, as a design question rather than an error:
  the scenario runs pair each SSP's spatial pattern with IHME's SSP2-like
  national population and mortality. Using the SSP2-4.5 pattern everywhere
  would make the population side fully SSP2 (consistent with IHME) and leave
  temperature as the only scenario difference; the current choice lets the
  SSP's urbanization and migration pattern shift exposure within a country.
  Neither is wrong; the report should say which one was used.
