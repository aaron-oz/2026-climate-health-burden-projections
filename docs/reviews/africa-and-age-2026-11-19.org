* Where age enters the analysis
Not in the temperature risk itself:
- Risk curves: the 17 cause-specific relative-risk curves have no age
  dimension. Each file (for example data/erf/ckd_curve_samples.csv) has only
  annual mean temperature (the zone), daily temperature and the draws. So,
  for a given cause, the model assumes the same relative risk from a hot or
  cold day at every age. (Believed to match Burkart et al., who did not
  estimate age-specific curves; the paper was not re-checked.)
- Minimum-risk temperature (TMREL): global-scripts/util_derive_tmrel.R
  weights the 17 curves by each cause's share of deaths summed over all ages
  and both sexes (line 55). Age structure affects it only through the cause
  mix.
- Attributable fraction (PAF): computed per year, cause and draw from daily
  temperatures; the same for every age and sex within a country, year, cause
  and draw (05_compute_pafs.R, comments at lines 257-266, "the merge
  broadcasts paf across age and sex").
- Exposure population: the gridded population maps used to weight grid cells
  (CCKP gpw-v4-rev11-<ssp>, 0.25 degree) carry one total-population value per
  cell, no age or sex (03_load_temperature.R requires pixel_id, date,
  daily_temp, pop; line 49). So every age group in a country gets the same
  temperature exposure.

Where age does enter:
1. Attributable deaths = IHME forecast deaths by cause, age, sex and draw x
   PAF x the scenario scale factor S = (1 - PAF_SSP2-4.5) / (1 - PAF_scenario),
   also per cause and draw (util_workflow_b_ratio.R). Within a cause,
   attributable deaths split across ages exactly as IHME's deaths do; heat
   and cold differ in age profile only through the cause mix (injuries are
   younger, with negative cold values).
2. YLLs = attributable deaths x remaining life expectancy at the start of the
   5-year age group, UN WPP national life tables by sex and year
   (07_compute_ylls.R). No mid-group adjustment in production (likely
   overstates YLLs per death somewhat; not measured). The Colombia validation
   mode used ex - 2.5 and 10 years for 80+, following Samuel's code.
3. Rates: IHME population by age and sex as denominators; age-standardized
   rates in the final pass reweight these by the GBD world standard.
4. Age breakdowns: sums over IHME's 5-year age groups.

Consequence: aging affects the projections only through IHME's forecast
(older populations die more of the causes with steep curves). Crude rates
therefore rise with aging even where warming does nothing (world cold, SSP2-4.5:
17.65 per 100,000 in 2022 against 21.63 in 2041-2050), which is why the
final pass adds age-standardized rates.

* How many African countries could this be run on
Africa here = the 54 UN member states: 48 in the World Bank's Sub-Saharan
Africa region plus Algeria, Djibouti, Egypt, Libya, Morocco and Tunisia (in
its Middle East, North Africa, Afghanistan and Pakistan region). All 54 are
among the 204 GBD countries and all have complete results in all four
scenarios. (Western Sahara, Reunion, Mayotte and St Helena are not GBD
countries.)

Burden thresholds use IHME's forecast deaths per year, mean over 2022-2050,
from the SSP2-4.5 Workflow B bundle
(review/scenarios-2026-10-20/workflow_b_bundle/workflow_b/by_cause.csv;
IHME deaths are identical across climate models, checked). Counts only; no
filter is applied to any result.

Sum of the 8 non-injury diseases (ischemic heart disease, stroke,
hypertensive heart disease, cardiomyopathy, diabetes, chronic kidney
disease, lower respiratory infections, COPD), then filter (the version Aaron
asked for, 2026-11-19):
| Disease deaths per year at least | Kept of 54 | Dropped (disease deaths per year) |
|----------------------------------+------------+-----------------------------------|
| 100 or 500                       | 54         | none |
| 1,000 or 2,000                   | 52         | Seychelles (587), Sao Tome and Principe (731) |
| 5,000                            | 47         | also Cabo Verde (2,182), Comoros (2,509), Eswatini (4,090), Djibouti (4,191), Equatorial Guinea (4,674) |
| 10,000                           | 40         | also Guinea-Bissau (6,655), Gabon (6,991), Gambia (7,216), Lesotho (8,057), Botswana (8,355), Mauritius (8,619), Namibia (9,218) |
| 20,000                           | 36         | also Mauritania (13,003), Liberia (16,932), Central African Republic (18,571), Eritrea (19,895) |
Any cutoff from about 750 to 2,000 gives the same 52.

For comparison, all 17 causes summed: 54 kept at 500, 52 at 1,000, 49 at
5,000, 42 at 10,000. Requiring every one of the 17 causes to reach 10 deaths
per year keeps only 26, because forces of nature (inj_disaster) is tiny
almost everywhere (median 9 per year; the smallest cause in 49 of 54
countries); with the 8 diseases each required to reach 10, all 54 pass
(cardiomyopathy is the smallest disease everywhere).
