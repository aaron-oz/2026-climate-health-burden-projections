#+TITLE: Summary tables for the final run on the production machine
#+DATE: 2026-11-16

Aaron's ruling (2026-11-16): collect every summary the team can think of and
compute each as a complete table in one run, before the per-draw output is
deleted. Cost is accepted without measuring (the run re-reads all scenario
output, about 153 CPU-hours last time, plus an unmeasured but tolerable
amount for the interval calculations). The gridded-population test is
dropped (it was for Aaron's understanding only).

Why everything has to be listed now: from a summary table, means of any
aggregate can be added up exactly later, but uncertainty ranges cannot,
because cells share draws (draw d uses the same risk-curve and mortality draw
in every country, year, cause and age). Checked on the 2026-11-06 output:
low and middle income heat deaths, SSP2-4.5, 2041-2050, true 95% range
455,997 to 992,219 per year; summing the countries' bounds gives 389,739 to
1,102,029, and treating countries as independent gives 542,291 to 798,197.
Crude rates are the exception: population is held at the mean of IHME's
forecast draws, so a rate's range is the count's range divided by a fixed
number and can be computed later from any count table.

* Settings shared by every table
- Scenarios: SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 (reference), SSP3-7.0, SSP5-8.5.
- Climate models: the 24 common to all four scenarios, pooled with the 500
  draws (12,000 values per number).
- Measures: deaths and years of life lost (YLLs), each for heat, cold, and
  heat + cold (heat + cold needs its own row: its range cannot be built from
  the heat and cold ranges).
- Numbers per cell: mean, median, 95%, 80% and 66% ranges, lowest and highest
  climate-model mean.
- For SSP1-2.6, SSP3-7.0 and SSP5-8.5 also: the difference from SSP2-4.5
  taken draw by draw (mean, 95/80/66% ranges), the ratio of means, and how
  many of the 24 models have a higher model mean than under SSP2-4.5.
- Also carried: IHME's forecast deaths for the same cell (mean), and the
  attributable fraction (attributable deaths / IHME deaths) with its ranges.
- Population: mean of IHME's forecast draws for each cell, so crude rates can
  be formed exactly from any count.

* Units (where people are)
- Each of the 204 countries.
- World.
- Income groups (CLASS_2026_10_08.csv): high, upper middle, lower middle, low.
- Low and middle income combined.
- The seven World Bank regions.

* Periods
- Each single year, 2022 to 2050.
- Decades: 2022-2030, 2031-2040, 2041-2050.
- The whole period 2022-2050.

* Breakdowns (each is its own table for every unit and period)
- All ages, both sexes, all causes.
- By sex.
- By age group.
- By age group and sex.
- By cause.
- By cause and sex.
- By cause and age group.
- By cause, age group and sex (the full split).

Age groups: the 17 five-year groups (0-4, 5-9, ..., 75-79, 80+) and broad
groups: under 5, 5-14, 15-49, 50-69, 70+, and 65+.

Causes: the 17 causes and groups of them: cardiovascular (ischemic heart
disease, stroke, hypertensive heart disease, cardiomyopathy), respiratory
(COPD, lower respiratory infections), diabetes and kidney disease, and
injuries (the nine injury causes); also all causes without "forces of
nature" (inj_disaster), for the Haiti and Honduras asterisk.

* Age-standardized rates
- Deaths and YLLs per 100,000, heat, cold, heat + cold.
- Two standard populations, GBD world standard and WHO 2000-2025 (the choice
  is with Charlie); the older groups of each standard summed into 80+.
- For every unit and period; both sexes and each sex; all causes and each
  cause group.

* Other tables
- Heat overtakes cold, per country and scenario: the distribution of the
  crossing year (a straight line through yearly heat minus cold, fitted per
  model and draw), and the share of model-draw pairs with heat above cold in
  each decade; the share also for each group.
- Population-weighted annual mean temperature, per country, year, scenario
  and model (context for the burden numbers).
- Totals (all ages, causes, sexes) under the two other model sets for the
  sensitivity check: models with both the scenario and SSP2-4.5, and all
  available models; every unit, decades and whole period.

* Size
Largest table, every country by year by age by sex by cause: 13.7 million
cells per measure; with 6 measures and the differences from SSP2-4.5, about
one billion numbers, roughly 13 GB as uncompressed CSV (bytes-per-number
arithmetic, not measured). Everything else adds much less.
