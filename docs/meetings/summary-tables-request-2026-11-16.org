Why everything has to be listed now: from a summary table, means of any
aggregate can be added up exactly later, but uncertainty ranges cannot,
because cells share draws (draw d uses the same risk-curve and mortality draw
in every country, year, cause and age). Checked on the 2026-11-06 output:
low and middle income heat deaths, SSP2-4.5, 2041-2050, true 95% range
455,997 to 992,219 per year; summing the countries' bounds gives 389,739 to
1,102,029, and treating countries as independent gives 542,291 to 798,197.
Crude rates are the exception: population is held at the mean of IHME's
forecast draws, so a rate's range is the count's range divided by a fixed
number and can be computed later from any count table. Ratios of means can
also be formed later from the means.

The same applies to money: a monetary total (deaths or YLLs times a value per
country and year, possibly discounted, summed over countries and years) is a
weighted sum of cells, so its mean can be computed from these tables but its
uncertainty range cannot. A valid range on a monetary total needs either the
valuation inputs (value per death or per life-year by country and year,
discount rate) inside this run, or a per-draw copy of the output kept for
later (open question 2 below).

* A. Final publishable tables

** Settings shared by every table
- Scenarios: SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 (reference), SSP3-7.0, SSP5-8.5.
- Climate models: the 24 common to all four scenarios, pooled with the 500
  draws (12,000 values per number).
- Measures: deaths and years of life lost (YLLs), each for heat, cold, and
  heat + cold (heat + cold needs its own row: its range cannot be built from
  the heat and cold ranges).
- Numbers per cell: mean, and 95%, 80% and 66% uncertainty ranges.
- For SSP1-2.6, SSP3-7.0 and SSP5-8.5 also: the difference from SSP2-4.5
  taken draw by draw (mean and the three ranges); this is the burden added or
  avoided by a scenario, which a valuation of scenarios needs.
- Population (mean of IHME's forecast draws) and IHME's forecast deaths from
  the 17 causes (mean), for rates and context.

** Units
- Each of the 204 countries; the world; the four income groups
  (CLASS_2026_10_08.csv); low and middle income combined; the seven World
  Bank regions.

** Periods
- Decades: 2022-2030, 2031-2040, 2041-2050, and the whole period 2022-2050,
  for every table.
- Annual, 2022 to 2050: one table only, all ages, both sexes, all causes
  combined, with the settings shared by every table (all units, measures,
  statistics and scenario differences). Decided 2026-11-18.
- No 5-year periods.

** Breakdowns (each is its own table for every unit and period)
- All ages, both sexes, all causes; by sex; by age group; by age group and
  sex; by cause; by cause and sex; by cause and age group; by cause, age
  group and sex (the full split).
- Age groups: the 17 five-year groups (0-4, 5-9, ..., 75-79, 80+) and broad
  groups: under 5, 15-49, 15-64 (working age, for valuing lost
  productivity), and 65+ (50-69 dropped 2026-11-18).
- Causes: the 17 causes and the groups in the sent email: cardiovascular
  (cvd_ihd, cvd_stroke, cvd_htn, cvd_cmp); diabetes and kidney disease
  (diabetes, ckd); injuries (all nine inj_*); all causes except forces of
  nature (inj_disaster), for the Haiti and Honduras asterisk. The GBD injury
  subgroups and the all-non-injury group in the earlier working list were
  not in the sent email and are not computed (team added nothing,
  2026-11-18).

** Age-standardized rates
- Deaths and YLLs per 100,000, heat, cold, heat + cold.
- One standard population: the GBD (IHME) world standard, its groups above
  80 summed into 80+. WHO 2000-2025 dropped (2026-11-18).
- For every unit and period; both sexes and each sex; all causes and each
  cause group.

** Size
Largest table, every country by year by age by sex by cause: 13.7 million
cells per measure; 6 measures with 7 numbers each, plus 7 more for the
difference from SSP2-4.5 in three scenarios, about 1 billion numbers, roughly
13 GB as uncompressed CSV (bytes-per-number arithmetic, not measured).
With the 2026-11-18 decision (no single years in the split tables) the
same table has 4 periods instead of 29 years, so roughly 2 GB by the same
arithmetic; the annual totals table is small.

* B. Vetting and assessment quantities (for our own checks only; coarse resolution, decided 2026-11-18)
Proposed at the coarse level only (world, income groups, low and middle
income, regions, and country totals; decades; no age, sex or cause split):
- Median, and the lowest and highest climate-model mean.
- Between-model share of the pooled variance.
- Number of the 24 models with a higher model mean than under SSP2-4.5.
- Attributable fraction (attributable deaths / IHME deaths) with ranges.
- Heat overtakes cold: per country and scenario, the distribution of the
  crossing year (straight line through yearly heat minus cold, per model and
  draw), and the share of model-draw pairs with heat above cold per decade.
- Population-weighted annual mean temperature per country, year, scenario and
  model.

* C. Historical estimates in the same tables (decided 2026-11-18)
No figures from us; the historical estimates go into the tables so they can
be queried next to the projections. Source, as Aaron leans (not fully
discussed): the GBD 2019 round only (Burkart et al. 2021 companion data,
1990, 2010, 2019), the round our method follows. Format (Aaron, 2026-11-18):
- Measures: deaths only, as crude rate per 100,000, age-standardized rate
  (GBD world standard, the same standard as our projections), and percent of
  all deaths; heat, cold, heat + cold; mean and GBD's 95% range (no 66% or
  80% range in the source). No YLLs.
- Places: countries and world as published; World Bank income groups and
  regions built from countries using GBD 2019-round population for 1990,
  2010, 2019 (if that population file is easy to obtain; otherwise
  countries and world only). Group values are means only, since ranges
  cannot be summed across countries.
- Shape: the same long table as the projections, with a source column
  ("GBD 2019 round" against each projection scenario), so one query returns
  both; empty 66% and 80% columns for GBD rows.
