* Decisions made, and why
- Headline model set: the 24 models common to all four scenarios, plus a
  sensitivity analysis across model-set options (common / paired / all
  available). Team, 2026-10-27; Aaron prefers one number per quantity.
- Keep the Workflow B scaling for headline numbers (team, 2026-10-27). It
  moves global totals by under 0.15%.
- Show heat, cold and net side by side, plus breakdowns by cause (team
  priority) and by age group (under 5, 15-49, 65+; by sex). Team and Aaron,
  2026-10-27.
- Measures: deaths, YLLs, rates per 100,000. YLLs keep our national
  definition (deaths x the country's UN WPP remaining life expectancy at the
  start of the 5-year age group; 80+ uses age 80). Not comparable with GBD's
  YLLs, which use a reference life table. Aaron skipped the GBD reference
  life table (GHDx download site down, 2026-10-28).
- Uncertainty intervals: 66%, 80% and 95% (team, 2026-10-27).
- Results shown as trends and decade averages, not single years
  (interannual noise; Caspar's suggestion, team agreed).
- Export design "option B": yearly totals plus cause and age breakdowns in
  the six blocks (Aaron, 2026-10-28). Rejected: cause every year (about 74
  GB more), cause groups instead of 17 causes (forecloses per-cause
  results), pooling the breakdowns on Caspar's machine (needs his machine
  for every later change).
- 6 significant digits rather than 5 (Aaron): about 4 GB more (about 22
  against 18 GB, fixture extrapolation) for a 10x smaller rounding error
  (at most 5e-6 of each value).

* Tried and ruled out
- Labels (country, model, cause columns) are not what makes the export big:
  a cause file with only its four value columns is the same size (17.0
  against 17.2 bytes per row on the fixture). Not worth optimizing.
- Wide-by-draw layout (one row per key, 500 draw columns): no smaller than
  the long layout on the fixture (17.7 against 17.2 bytes per row at 6
  digits, gzip).
