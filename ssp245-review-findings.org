* The 2022 anchor against Burkart et al. 2021

** Global magnitude

Our 2022 global ensemble total is 1.684 million deaths attributable to
non-optimal temperature (356,686 heat + 1,327,087 cold) across the 204
locations. Burkart et al. 2021 report 1.69 million (95% UI 1.52 to 1.83) for
2019 (verified against the paper text held in the repo). The agreement is
consistent with the pipeline reproducing the published methodology at global
scale; it is not proof, since our number differs from theirs in temperature
source (CMIP6 models, no ERA5 baseline shift), mortality (IHME forecasts),
year (2022 vs 2019), and it is possible for offsetting errors to land near a
published total.

* Trend direction and shape, 2022 to 2050

Raw counts move the intuitive way for heat and the counterintuitive way for
cold: heat-attributable deaths rise in 197 of 204 locations, but
cold-attributable deaths ALSO rise in 184 of 204 (global cold total 1.33M in
2022 to 1.99M in 2050). Two composition effects, not climate, appear to
account for the cold rise:

1. Aging. Attributable shares standardized to fixed 2022 age-sex death
   weights flip the picture: the cold share rises in only 64 of 204
   locations after standardization (vs 141 crude), while the heat share
   rises in 197 of 204 either way. 77 locations flip from crude-rising to
   standardized-falling on cold.
2. Cause mix. The residual standardized cold rise concentrates at high
   latitude (30 of the 33 locations with |centroid latitude| > 45 deg).
   Decomposing that group by cause: within-cause cold shares are nearly
   flat (for example COPD 0.121 in 2022 to 0.119 in 2050), but the IHME
   mortality forecast shifts deaths toward cold-heavy causes: in the
   high-latitude group, chronic kidney disease deaths grow from 269k to
   687k, COPD 429k to 678k, LRI 263k to 407k, while ischemic heart disease
   and stroke shrink. Age-sex standardization cannot remove a cause-mix
   shift within age groups, so this passes through.

Taken together the climate signal itself behaves as expected: heat share up
almost everywhere, within-cause cold shares flat to slightly falling. This
also confirms the agenda's warning that projected burden counts mix the
mortality forecast with the climate signal; any headline trend statement
should be made on PAF-style shares (ideally age-standardized), not counts.

* Across-model spread

- No model dominates the ensemble. Using a robust z-score of each model's
  non-optimal deaths against the per-(location, year) ensemble median, the
  largest mean |z| is 1.27 (ukesm1-0-ll), and no model exceeds |z| > 5 in
  more than 0.8% of combos. The three warmest-tilted models (ukesm1-0-ll,
  hadgem3-gc31-ll, kace-1-0-g, mean z 0.42 to 0.54) are all models the
  climate literature flags as high-sensitivity, so their position is
  expected rather than suspicious.
- Location-level spread is modest for most of the grid: the across-model
  coefficient of variation (CV) of 2050 non-optimal deaths has median 0.09,
  but a long tail (95th percentile 0.58).
- The wide-spread tail is mostly the sub-grid locations (next section), plus
  a few resolved countries worth a look: Malaysia (CV 0.61; 2050 ensemble
  1,632 attributable deaths, model range 391 to 3,877), Suriname (CV 0.75),
  Brunei (CV 0.64), and Singapore, where the ensemble mean is -96 deaths
  with models disagreeing on sign (range -171 to +135). Near-zero
  non-optimal totals make CV explode arithmetically, so sign disagreement
  rather than CV is the meaningful flag for these.

flag them in any headline table.

* How often the temperature-zone clamp binds

Run 2026-08-17 (=analysis5_clamp.R=). The pipeline assigns each pixel a
temperature zone equal to its rounded annual mean daily temperature, clamped
to [6, 28] deg C, and the ERF curves exist only for those zones. This
analysis measures the share of population living in pixels whose rounded
annual mean falls outside that range, i.e. where the zone assignment is
clamped, from the global CCKP daily tas NetCDFs for two models (access-cm2,
a mid-range model, and canesm5, a known high-sensitivity one) under ssp245.
What it does not measure: the burden impact of the clamp. A clamped pixel
still gets a burden (evaluated on the zone-28 curve with daily temperatures
truncated to that zone's modelled range); the direction of the error for
heat is expected negative, its magnitude is not quantified here. The
second clamp mechanism (within-zone daily truncation) is also not measured.

#+caption: Share of population in pixels with rounded annual mean outside 6 to 28 deg C, ssp245. Pop weights: GPW gridded population, 2020-2039 climatology for 2022 and 2040-2059 for 2050.
| model      | year | hot side (>28) | cold side (<6) |
|------------+------+----------------+----------------|
| access-cm2 | 2022 |          7.9% |           1.8% |
| access-cm2 | 2050 |         14.9% |           0.9% |
| canesm5    | 2022 |          7.8% |           1.9% |
| canesm5    | 2050 |         23.5% |           0.8% |

- The clamp is already a first-order feature for a band of countries in
  2022, not a hot-tail hypothetical: 14 locations have more than half their
  population in hot-clamped pixels (Niger 97%, Burkina Faso 95%, Djibouti
  94%, Cambodia 92%, Chad 91%, Mali 88%, UAE 84%, Sudan 79%, South Sudan
  73%, Mauritania 71%, Oman 69%, ...), rising to 22 locations by 2050.
- Overlap with the GBD hot-belt shortfall is substantial but not
  deterministic: Niger, Chad, Mali, Sudan and South Sudan top both lists,
  and the Spearman correlation between 2022 clamp share and the ours/GBD
  ratio is -0.325 (more clamp, lower ratio) over locations with more than
  100 GBD attributable deaths. But Cambodia (92% clamped, ratio 1.73),
  Guinea-Bissau (1.47) and Senegal (1.10) run HIGH against GBD despite
  heavy clamping, so the clamp is at most one of several drivers of the
  hot-belt disagreement, alongside the missing ERA5 baseline shift and
  GBD-round revisions.
- Under ssp245 to 2050 the hot-side clamp roughly doubles to triples in
  population terms (model-dependent; canesm5's 23.5% reflects its high
  climate sensitivity). Under ssp585 to 2100 it would be much larger. Every
  degree of warming above zone 28 is a degree the burden estimate cannot
  see, so this needs an explicit methods statement, and ideally a
  sensitivity analysis, before hot-end results are published. Two-model
  sample; the full 27-model version is a cheap addition to a production-side
  pass on Caspar's machine.

* Separating the hot-belt shortfall drivers

Run 2026-08-17 (=analysis6_shortfall.R=). Four tests against the candidate
explanations for the hot-belt shortfall vs GBD. Summary first: the gap is
almost entirely on the PAF side, not the mortality side; within the PAF
side, within-zone truncation of hot days is the leading pipeline-side
mechanism for the Gulf and Sahel, it cannot explain Haiti, temperature bias
is ruled out where testable, observed-2022 weather is ruled out, and a
GBD-round revision concentrated in hot countries remains consistent with
everything but is only indirectly measurable.

** The gap decomposes onto the PAF side

ours/GBD attributable deaths = (ours/GBD 17-cause total deaths) x (ours/GBD
attributable fraction). Globally the mortality inputs agree (17-cause deaths
ratio 1.022; per-location median 0.99), so the burden gap is not a
mortality-forecast artifact in aggregate. For the shortfall countries:

** Within-zone truncation (large for Gulf/Sahel, absent for Haiti)

The ERF for the hottest zone is only defined up to a daily mean of 32.1 deg
C; hotter days are evaluated at that endpoint. The pop-weighted share of
person-days above the pixel's zone maximum (access-cm2, 2022): UAE 40.2%,
Kuwait 37.9% (mean exceedance 5.5 deg C), Oman 37.0%, Qatar 36.9%, Sudan
32.4%, Iraq 32.1%, Niger 32.0%, Mali 30.4%, Pakistan 30.0%, Chad 25.7%;
India 16.8% (mean exceedance 3.2 deg C); Nigeria 8.9%; Cambodia 6.9%; Haiti
0.0%. Spearman correlation with the burden ratio is -0.309. By 2050
(canesm5) the top values reach the mid-40s%. A quarter to a third of hot
exposure being flat-capped is mechanically capable of producing PAF ratios
of the size observed in the Gulf and Sahel, but the burden actually lost is
still not quantified (the endpoint RR is applied, not zero), and Haiti
shows the shortfall exists with no truncation at all.

** Temperature bias: ruled out where testable

Pop-weighted comparison of the two CMIP6 models against ERA5 observed 2022
(hourly, aggregated to daily means) for the seven countries with usable
local ERA5: annual-mean biases are +0.1 to +0.9 deg C for five of them,
with two warm outliers (Colombia +2.8 to +2.9, Philippines +1.8 to +1.9);
none is cool-biased. For India specifically the models see MORE hot-tail
than ERA5 (13.9% and 12.4% of days above 32.1 deg C vs 10.1% observed), so
the missing CMIP6-to-ERA5 baseline shift does not explain the Indian
shortfall. Caveat: no Gulf or Sahel country has local ERA5, so the
truncation-heavy group is untested for bias, and Mexico and Italy dropped
out of this check on a data handling step that was not chased.

** Observed-2022 weather: ruled out

GBD's own series is flat or falling 2019 to 2022 in the key countries
(India 0.99, Niger 0.97, Pakistan 0.94 on non-optimal; Sudan is the
exception at 1.29), so the shortfall is not our climatological model year
missing a real 2022 heatwave.

** GBD-round revision: indirect but consistent

Measured directly on the nine Burkart countries (this round's 2019 vs the
paper's 2019), revisions are modest and two-directional: non-optimal 0.88
(China) to 1.19 (South Africa). Yet the global 2019 total rose 9% (1.69M to
1.84M), which arithmetically places the upward revision outside these nine
temperate-and-large countries, i.e., disproportionately in the hot belt.
The shortfall countries are not in the paper's table, so their revision
cannot be measured directly; on this evidence the revision explanation
stays plausible and unquantified. Per-country factors:
=output/review-ssp245/gbd_round_revision_9countries.csv=.

** The truncation experiment, small version (run 2026-08-17)

=analysis7_experiments.R= replicates the pipeline's summary-mode PAF stage
(05_compute_pafs.R: RR rescaled to the TMREL bin, per-bin contribution
pr x (RR-1)/RR, heat = bins above TMREL) at national level for
access-cm2-r1i1p1f1 / 2022, and validates it against the production run's
own per-cause results for the same combo: correlation 0.993, median
absolute PAF difference 0.002 (residual differences trace to the replica
using summary curves where production used 500 draws). The heat side is
then re-evaluated with log(RR) extended log-linearly past the zone max
(slope fitted to the last 0.5 deg C of each curve) instead of capped.

Niger and Sudan, the mortality-input undercount.

** Haiti and Cambodia case-study diagnostics

From the same replica (pop-weighted daily-temperature statistics,
access-cm2 2022):

- Haiti: modal zone 24, TMREL 25.4 deg C, daily temperature P05/P50/P95 =
  22.1 / 25.2 / 28.3 deg C. The whole distribution spans about 6 deg C
  with the TMREL near its middle, so both tails are short and the PAF is
  small by construction; truncation is exactly zero. Haiti's shortfall
  therefore traces to exposure spread around the TMREL, not to the clamp;
  with 39 coastal grid cells this connects to the ocean-damped-variance
  concern from the sub-grid section rather than to the ERF domain.
- Cambodia: modal zone 28, TMREL 26.5 deg C, P50 = 29.6 deg C; 88.8% of
  person-days sit above the TMREL, so nearly the entire year is "heat" and
  the PAF is large, putting us 73% above GBD. With the TMREL that far into
  the left tail, small differences in TMREL placement or exposure produce
  large burden swings; TMREL placement is the variable to scrutinize for
  Cambodia-like profiles.

Both diagnoses are descriptive: without GBD's own exposure and TMREL for
these countries we cannot say which side is closer to truth.

** Note on who truncates

Checked 2026-08-17 in IHME's own released code
(=from-samuel/Info Burkart/environmental_risk_factors-temperature_lancet_2021/pafCalc_sevFix.R=,
lines 139-144): the paper-era GBD pipeline clamps the zone to [6, 28] and
each day to the zone's [minTemp, maxTemp], exactly as our
=03_load_temperature.R= does. Truncation is therefore not a deviation we
introduced; it is the paper-era method, faithfully reproduced. Whether the
current GBD round still truncates is unknown to us; its large upward
hot-belt revisions (next section) would be consistent with an extended or
re-estimated hot tail, which is what our extrapolation experiment
approximates.

* Resolution: the round-revision measurement (2026-08-17)

Aaron downloaded the paper-era companion dataset (GHDx "Mortality Burden
Attributable to Non-Optimal Temperature 1990-2019", DOI 10.6069/7QWW-PF74)
and =analysis8_revision.R= compares all 204 locations against it. The file
carries per-person rates and PAFs, not counts; counts were reconstructed
using population implied by the current-round download (rate x population;
a PAF-based reconstruction agrees, see the script output). Headline:

- Against the paper era, our 2022 global total is 0.999 of their 2019
  value; Spearman rank correlation 0.993; 159 of 204 locations fall inside
  the paper's 95% UI; median ratio 0.94 among the 154 locations with more
  than 100 paper-era deaths. (Ours is 2022 vs their 2019, but GBD's own
  2019-to-2022 movement is small, so the comparison is meaningful.)
- Every hot-belt shortfall country except UAE lands inside the paper-era
  UI: Niger 0.84, Sudan 1.31, Kuwait 1.12, Iraq 0.94, India 0.96, Chad
  1.07, Mali 0.97, Oman 0.98, Pakistan 0.90, Mauritania 1.30. Their
  current-round revision factors are 1.3 to 2.3 (Sudan 2.34, Niger 2.27,
  Kuwait 2.24, India 1.67; heat-side Iraq reaches 2.68). The hot-belt
  shortfall vs the current round is therefore, for these countries, GBD
  round revision, not a pipeline defect: the pipeline reproduces the
  methodology it implements.
- Cambodia resolves the other way: ours 1.04 vs paper era, and its
  current-round 2019 value (2,114) also matches; the 1.73 came from GBD's
  own estimate dropping 42% between 2019 and 2022 within the current
  round. Nothing on our side to fix; worth understanding their 2022 drop
  before quoting Cambodia.
- YLLs: paper-era global YLL per attributable death is 19.4 years vs our
  13.9 (current round 21.2), so the YLL gap persists against both rounds
  and remains consistent with the reference-life-table difference; it
  should be stated as definitional wherever YLLs are compared.

** Verification of the comparison dataset (added later on 2026-08-17)

Cross-checking the GHDx companion dataset against the paper's own Table 1
for the nine countries: PAFs agree to at most 0.015 percentage points with
digit-identical uncertainty intervals, so the dataset is the paper's
estimates, verified numerically. The count reconstruction (per-person rate
x current-round implied population) reproduces Table 1 death counts within
+/-7% for seven of nine countries (Guatemala 0.88, New Zealand 1.10), so
per-location count ratios above carry roughly +/-10% reconstruction noise;
PAF-based comparisons are exact.

** Composition of the 45 locations outside the paper-era UI

Of the 45 (of 204) locations whose 2022 ensemble falls outside the
paper-era 95% UI: 41 are low and 4 are high. The lows split into the 23
with more than 50 paper-era deaths discussed above (20 of them tropical)
plus 18 with at most 50 paper-era deaths, and those 18 are ALL tropical
and nearly all small islands (Solomon Islands, Fiji, Timor-Leste, Cape
Verde, Maldives, Kiribati, ...; 11 of the 45 are sub-grid locations),
uniformly at 0.25 to 0.65 of the paper value. The 4 highs (The Gambia
1.60, Saudi Arabia 1.38, Thailand 1.35, Ecuador 1.34) fit neither
mechanism below. 28 of the 45 sit within 15% of a UI bound, close enough
that the ~10% count-reconstruction noise plus the 2022-vs-2019 year
mismatch plausibly account for them; a perfect reimplementation would not
reach 95% coverage on this comparison anyway, since ours is climate-model
2022 against their observed-weather 2019.

** Exposure provenance, and the ERA5 re-referencing test

The CCKP daily product the production run consumes is NASA NEX-GDDP-CMIP6
(verified from the NetCDF =wb_*= attributes; Thrasher et al. 2022): CMIP6
already bias-corrected and downscaled by quantile mapping against GMFD
(the Princeton Global Meteorological Forcing Dataset) over 1960-2014. So
the pipeline's exposure is neither raw CMIP6 nor ERA5, and the "tropical
variance deficit" measured earlier is more precisely a NEX/GMFD-vs-ERA5
reference disagreement (the paper's exposure was ERA5-based).

A local re-referencing test (=analysis10_qm.R=: per-pixel empirical
quantile mapping of the 2022 CCKP series onto 2022 ERA5, through the
validated replica) gives a mixed verdict:

#+caption: Replica non-optimal burden (deaths) under three exposure variants vs the paper-era value. access-cm2, 2022; single-year QM conflates weather with climatology.
| country     |   raw | mean-shift | QM to ERA5 | paper [95% UI]      |
|-------------+-------+------------+------------+---------------------|
| Rwanda      |   765 |        893 |        900 | 937 [771, 1150]     |
| Philippines | 3,964 |      2,473 |      2,125 | 5,180 [3,891, 6,878] |
| Costa Rica  |   267 |        398 |        395 | 245 [179, 318]      |
| Colombia    | 4,917 |      4,989 |      5,008 | 4,986 [3,796, 6,360] |

Re-referencing fixes Rwanda, leaves the control (Colombia) unchanged,
overshoots Costa Rica, and moves the Philippines away from the paper
value. On this evidence ERA5 re-referencing is NOT supported as a blanket
production change; the mechanism is real in places but country-specific
structure dominates, and a proper test would need multi-year correction
windows (roughly 6 GB of additional CCKP years, feasible locally).

** UAE: exposure ruled out, gap unexplained but within joint uncertainty

With ERA5 2022 for the UAE fetched from the public ARCO-ERA5 archive
(=fetch_uae_era5.py=), the CCKP exposure there is close to unbiased:
pop-weighted mean 28.6/29.1 deg C (two models) vs 29.0 observed, P95
within 0.7 deg C, share of days above 32.1 deg C 40/45% vs 38% observed.
Re-referencing to ERA5 moves the replica burden from 834 to 824-850,
nowhere near the paper's 1,520. So the UAE's point-estimate gap (~0.5 vs
both GBD vintages) is not exposure, not mortality inputs (ours are 56%
higher), not the truncation rule (shared with the paper), and not the
curves or TMRELs (same released files). It remains unexplained at the
point-estimate level; our draw interval [431, 1184] does overlap the
paper's UI [958, 2165], so the two are not statistically inconsistent.

23 locations with more than 50 paper-era deaths sit below the paper-era
95% UI (=revision_all204.csv=): Singapore, Haiti (0.35), Malaysia (0.45),
UAE (0.52), Uganda, Indonesia (0.56), Mauritius, Namibia, Papua New
Guinea, Botswana, Tanzania, Honduras, DR Congo, Malta, Zambia, Mozambique,
Nicaragua, Malawi, Zimbabwe, Georgia, Ethiopia, Portugal, Brazil (0.86).
19 of the 23 are tropical or subtropical, including large well-resolved
countries (Indonesia has 2,454 grid cells), so this is not the sub-grid
island problem; it looks like damped tropical daily variance in CMIP6.
Local tests (=analysis9_variance.R=):

- Within-pixel daily SD, ERA5 vs CMIP6, for the ERA5-covered countries:
  in the two low-variance tropical countries CMIP6 underestimates the
  daily SD by roughly 30 to 40% (Rwanda: ERA5 1.04 deg C vs 0.71/0.74 for
  the two models; Philippines: 1.17 vs 0.82/0.68), while mid- and
  high-variance countries are close to unbiased (Bangladesh 0.94, South
  Africa 0.91 to 0.96, India 0.91 to 1.04, Colombia 0.94 to 1.10). Seven
  countries, two models, one year: suggestive of a tropical variance
  deficit, not proof.
- Haiti's paper-era value (416) lies inside our production draw interval
  ([-1329, 510]), so the Haiti discrepancy is within our stated
  uncertainty; the point estimate is low, not inconsistent.
- Variance-inflation sensitivity in the replica: scaling daily anomalies
  by about 1.2 to 1.25 reproduces the paper-era Haiti burden. Treat as
  order-of-magnitude only: the replica (summary curves, one model)
  overstates near-zero-burden countries relative to production, and its
  Indonesia run is not validated.

* YLLs per attributable death

For locations with |non-optimal deaths| > 1 in 2022, the ratio of YLLs to
attributable deaths has median 15.0 years, interquartile range 11.4 to 23.3
years, which sits in the plausible band for remaining life expectancy at the
ages where temperature-attributable deaths occur. Two tails worth knowing:

- High end (37 to 51 years): Somalia (37.1, on 1,329 attributable deaths),
  Cote d'Ivoire (37.3), El Salvador (37.9), Equatorial Guinea (48.8, on 3.8
  deaths), Solomon Islands (50.6, on 5.0 deaths). Ratios near 40 years imply
  attributable deaths concentrated at young ages, which is plausible where
  child LRI or young-adult chronic kidney disease carries the burden, but we
  have not verified the cause-age split for these; worth one targeted look
  before publishing them.
- Negative ratios at near-zero burdens: Cape Verde (-8.3, on 10.2 deaths),
  Timor-Leste (-2.1), Greenland (-1.6). Deaths and YLL aggregates take
  opposite signs when heat- and cold-attributable parts nearly cancel and
  their age profiles differ. Small-number artifact, not a bug; these
  locations' net burdens are statistically indistinguishable from zero.
